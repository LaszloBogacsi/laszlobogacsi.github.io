## CS degree or self-taught?

When you're deciding on this new and exciting chapter of your life, perhaps the most frequently asked question is, "..Do I need to have a computer science degree..?"

I don't intend to dive deep into the controversial question of "..is having a diploma in something, actually worth it?" There are many writings out there analysing this topic from many points:

- Theoretical vs practical approach
- Academic learning vs applied hands-on learning
- Financial perspectives, student loan + invested/unpaid years of a university vs investing all your free time and learn while on the job, and make money in the meantime.
- Transferable and general skills from any uni course vs specialising early on
- Opportunities with or without "a good name", prestige or paper.

This list could grow quite long. As a personal opinion coming from a civil/structural engineering background, there are invaluable skills that a uni can teach you and they are not necessarily close to the subjects. I'm thinking of problem solving, analysing and breaking down a task, grit, perseverance, patience, precision, learning methods.

Years in uni can teach you a lot about yourself, you'll explore your limits and how to overstep them, you'll learn what works best for you.

Having any other degree helps but is not essential, it depends so much on the person.

So do you need a degree in CS? No. But eventually you'll need the knowledge. This is a tradeoff of investing time and money early on vs. later.

I'm a bootcamp "graduate" and a self-taught developer.

A bootcamp can give you only so much, it's short. You only get as much out of it as much as you put in. And there are many different approaches, you might be an entrepreneur looking to learn some basics, you might just be looking for inspiration and ideas, or like me you want to become a developer, but have a 1000 questions. A bootcamp can answer most of them.

I wanted to know if this is for me or not, this can give you the feeling and a good experience. All I was looking for was a reassurance – not having written a single line of code before – if this is something I could do for the foreseeable. Does this have the potential to grow and keep me motivated and excited for a long time? And just in general: am I able to code?

For me the answer was yes.
